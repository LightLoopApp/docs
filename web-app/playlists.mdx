A playlist is an ordered sequence of media items. It's the core building block of LightLoop — playlists go into feeds, feeds go to Apple TVs.

## Creating a playlist

1. Click the **Playlists** tab
2. Click **New Playlist**
3. Enter a name
4. Optionally upload or select **cover art** (appears as the playlist thumbnail on Apple TV)
5. Click **Create**

{/* SCREENSHOT PLACEHOLDER: New playlist dialog with the name field and cover art upload area */}

## Adding items to a playlist

Inside the playlist editor, click **Add Items** to open a media picker. Select one or more items from your library and click **Add**.

Items appear in the order you add them. Drag the handle on the left side of any item to reorder.

{/* SCREENSHOT PLACEHOLDER: Media picker modal showing a grid of thumbnails with checkboxes, and 2 items already selected */}

## Reordering and removing items

- **Drag** the handle icon to reorder
- **Click the eye icon** to hide an item without removing it (it won't play but stays in the list)
- **Click the trash icon** to remove an item from this playlist (doesn't delete the file from your library)

{/* SCREENSHOT PLACEHOLDER: Close-up of a playlist item row showing the drag handle, eye/hide toggle, and remove button */}

## Hiding items

Toggling an item's visibility is useful during a live service when you want to skip content without permanently removing it. Hidden items appear grayed out in the list but are ignored during playback.

## Auto-advance

By default, playlists auto-advance through items automatically. Under **Playlist Settings** you can configure:

| Setting | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Auto-Advance Video/Audio** | Toggle on/off. When off, playback pauses at the end of each item and waits for a manual tap on the Siri Remote. |
| **Transition (sec)** | The crossfade duration in seconds between items (0 = instant cut, up to 5 seconds). |
| **Looping Slideshow** | **Disabled** by default. Choose 1–20 seconds to make static items (images, graphics, web, presentations) advance on a timer and loop through the playlist. |

{/* SCREENSHOT PLACEHOLDER: Playlist settings panel showing the Auto-Advance toggle, Transition field, and Looping Slideshow picker */}

## Looping Slideshow

Videos and audio advance on their own when the file ends, but static items — images, graphics, web pages, presentations, and songs without an audio track — have no natural end. **Looping Slideshow** is what makes them advance.

- **Disabled** (the default) — each static item stays on screen until you advance it manually with the Siri Remote. Use this for a single hold slide, a sermon graphic, or a countdown you control by hand.
- **1–20 seconds** — each static item holds for that many seconds, then advances to the next item. When the playlist reaches the end it loops back to the start, so the playlist runs unattended.

<Note>
  Looping Slideshow is a playlist-wide setting on Apple TV — every static item in the playlist uses the same interval. It has no effect on videos or audio, which always play to the end of the file.
</Note>

## Soundtracks

A soundtrack is an audio-only playlist that plays as a background layer — music or ambient sound behind your main visual content.

To add a soundtrack:

1. Open a playlist
2. Under **Soundtrack**, click **Choose Soundtrack**
3. Select an audio-only playlist from your library
4. Adjust the **volume** (0–100%)
5. Toggle **Loop** (repeats the soundtrack) or **Shuffle** (plays tracks in random order)

{/* SCREENSHOT PLACEHOLDER: Soundtrack section showing a selected audio playlist, volume slider at 60%, and Loop/Shuffle toggles */}

The soundtrack plays independently — it doesn't stop when visual items transition, and it loops seamlessly when the playlist restarts.

<Tip>
  Create a dedicated "Background Music" playlist with only audio files, then reference it as the soundtrack for your main visual playlists.
</Tip>

### Item-level soundtrack overrides

Individual items within a playlist can trigger their own soundtrack when they come on screen — overriding the playlist-wide soundtrack for just that item.

**Example use cases:**
- Switch to a specific song's instrumental when that announcement slide appears
- Silence music during a video item that has its own audio
- Play an upbeat track only during a specific Welcome Party slide

To set an item-level soundtrack:

1. In the playlist editor, click the item to expand its options
2. Under **Action Soundtrack**, click **Choose**
3. Select an audio-only playlist
4. The chosen soundtrack starts playing when this item appears and hands back to the playlist soundtrack when the item ends

{/* SCREENSHOT PLACEHOLDER: Expanded playlist item row showing the "Action Soundtrack" section with a selected audio playlist and a Clear button */}

<Note>
  If an item has no action soundtrack set, the playlist-wide soundtrack continues playing uninterrupted through that item.
</Note>

## Countdown banner

A playlist item can put a **countdown banner** on screen when it becomes current — the "Service begins in 5:00" bar across a pre-service loop. It draws above everything else, including overlays.

To add one:

1. In the playlist editor, open an item's **Actions**
2. Next to **Countdown Banner**, click **Start** (**Stop** on a later item clears a running countdown; **None** leaves it alone)
3. Choose how it counts:
   - **Static** — a fixed duration in minutes and seconds, starting when the item appears
   - **To Time** — counts down to a clock time, e.g. `10:30`, however long that takes

{/* SCREENSHOT PLACEHOLDER: Item Actions modal Countdown Banner section set to Start, showing Type / Duration / Position / Display and the styling controls */}

### Where it shows

**Display** decides who sees it:

- **Program** — the audience output, and optionally mirrored onto a TV Wall Stage quadrant
- **Stage Only** — a speaker's timer shown only on stage displays (the iOS Remote's Stage Mode and the Apple TV's TV Wall Stage Display), never on the audience screen. See [Stage countdown timer](/ios-remote/stage-mode#stage-countdown-timer).

**Position** places the bar at the **top** or **bottom** of the screen.

### Styling

| Setting | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Background** | **Gradient** (with start and end colors), a solid **Color**, or **None** for text straight over the content |
| **Pretext** | The words before the timer — defaults to `Begins in: ` |
| **Colors** | Separate colors for the pretext and the timer digits |
| **Font / Size / Align** | Typeface, point size, and left/center/right alignment |

### On Complete

What the banner does when it reaches zero:

- **Hide Banner** (default) — the banner fades away and the item keeps playing
- **Allow Overrun** — the timer keeps counting *past* zero into negative time, in your chosen **Overrun Color**, so a speaker can see how far over they are

### Automation

The countdown can also drive the screen when it reaches zero:

| Option | What happens at zero |
| --- | --- |
| **None** (default) | Nothing — the **On Complete** behavior applies |
| **Playlist** | The chosen playlist goes live, exactly as if you had switched to it |
| **Next Slide** | The screen advances one step, exactly as if an operator had pressed **Next** — the next slide of a song if one is playing, otherwise the next item in the playlist |

**Next Slide** is the simplest way to let a hold slide time out on its own: put a countdown on a "service begins in 5:00" slide and let it hand over to whatever comes next, with no second playlist to build.

<Note>
  Choosing a **Playlist** here adds it to every feed that contains this playlist, so the Apple TV already has it downloaded and can switch instantly. Both automations override **On Complete** — the banner clears as the screen moves.
</Note>

<Note>
  **Next Slide** requires the Apple TV app **1.3.6** or later. Earlier versions ignore it and fall back to the **On Complete** setting.
</Note>

## Overlays

A playlist item can show an **overlay** — a small layer that floats in a corner above the content (above everything except a countdown banner). Three types are available:

- **Watermark** — an image from your Media Library (logo, sponsor mark, "live" bug), with adjustable opacity.
- **QR Code** — generated from any URL, with custom foreground/background colors and an optional caption (e.g. "Scan to give").
- **Weather** — current conditions for a US ZIP code (°F), refreshed every 60 seconds while shown.

To add an overlay:

1. In the playlist editor, open an item's **Actions**.
2. Under **Overlay**, choose **Show** (or **Hide** to clear one).
3. Pick the **Duration**:
   - **This item** — the overlay shows only while this item is on screen.
   - **Until cleared** — the overlay persists across following items until an item with an Overlay **Hide** action.
4. Choose the **Type** and fill in its settings, then set the **Corner**, **Size**, and **Padding**.

{/* SCREENSHOT PLACEHOLDER: Item Actions modal Overlay section set to Show / Until cleared / Watermark, with the searchable image grid and corner/size/padding controls */}

Overlays render on Apple TV (including TV Wall **Program** output) and on the shared preview page.

<Tip>
  Use **Until cleared** with a watermark to keep your church logo or a sponsor mark on screen across an entire playlist, then drop a **Hide** overlay action on the item where it should disappear.
</Tip>

## Sharing a playlist for review

Before a service, share a read-only preview link with your team, pastor, or worship leader for approval.

1. Open the playlist
2. Click **Share**
3. Copy the **Public Preview Link**

Anyone with the link can see the playlist contents and order without signing in. They cannot make changes.

{/* SCREENSHOT PLACEHOLDER: Playlist share modal with a copyable link and a QR code */}

## Sharing a playlist with another LightLoop account

The Share modal also has a **Share with Another LightLoop Account** section for sending a playlist to a different organization. The recipient clicks the link, signs in, and the playlist is added to their account. An optional expiry date automatically removes the content after the event ends.

See [Sharing](/web-app/sharing) for full details.

## Adding Playlist to a Feed

1. Open the playlist
2. Click **Add to Feed**
3. Select a Feed to add the playlist to

## Playlist groups

Groups are folders that organize related playlists in the sidebar. For example: `Series: Summer 2025`, `Kidddo`, `Pre-Service`.

### Creating a group

1. In the Playlists sidebar, click **New Group**
2. Name the group
3. Drag playlists into it (or drag them out to move them back to the top level)

{/* SCREENSHOT PLACEHOLDER: Playlists sidebar with two groups shown — one collapsed and one expanded showing 3 playlists inside */}

Groups are visual only — they don't affect playback order on Apple TV.

## Deleting a playlist

Open the playlist, scroll to **Danger Zone**, and click **Delete Playlist**. Confirm the deletion.

<Warning>
  Deleting a playlist removes it from any feeds it belongs to. Connected Apple TVs will receive the update and purge the cached files for that playlist.
</Warning>
